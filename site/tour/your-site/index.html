The starting point

One person, and one minute on a phone

Who Lee-Elle is, in her words

  • South African-born and Israel-raised, a fluent Hebrew speaker who later moved to the UK
  • Teaches Hebrew to children, teenagers and adults, in London and on Zoom
  • Experience with children aged 7 to 11; Enhanced DBS checked
  • First Class Honours BA, Guildford School of Acting: an actor-musician who also teaches singing, trombone and guitar

What the site has to do

  • A parent arrives on a phone, usually from a recommendation, and decides in about a minute
  • They look for: who teaches, what ages, where, whether it is safe
  • So every design puts her photo, the lessons and the facts on the first screen, and one action: ask about lessons

Design 1 · Alef-bet

Her photo, seen through the first letter

Why it suits Lee-Elle

  • Hebrew starts with alef. She teaches complete beginners and reading and writing, so the page is built from the alphabet.
  • Her photo inside the alef says, before a word is read: this is the teacher who opens the language.
  • All 22 letters arrive across the page, right to left; every section opens on its own letter, with its English name.

A parent of a young beginner sees a teacher and the letters their child will learn.

Design 3 · Three tenses

was → is → will be

Why it suits Lee-Elle

  • She teaches past, present and future tenses, so the page is organised by them.
  • Past is her story, in sepia: South Africa, Israel, the UK, Guildford. Present is lessons today, in colour. Future is an empty frame marked "you".
  • The headline is grammar itself; the page ends on the student's goal and the button.

How to choose

Five questions, one for each design

  • Should visitors meet the language first? Alef-bet
  • Should a parent's questions be answered one by one? Conversation
  • Should the page show a path, from first lesson to goal? Three tenses
  • Should they meet a teacher who performs? The stage
  • Just the facts, ready to print? Notebook

Every design, in Hebrew

עברית, in every single design

Not a translation pasted on: each of the five designs is rebuilt right to left, with Hebrew type, the layout mirrored, and the letters used as material. One switch at the top of every page changes the language, and the same design stays on screen.

  • Ten pages from one set of words: five designs, two languages
  • For Hebrew-speaking parents in the UK, and for children growing up between two languages
  • The translation is marked on every page as not yet checked by her, until she has corrected it

What we can do

A sovereign website, made for one person

  • Five ideas from one textFive designs, each from a different part of her life, all from the words she supplied
  • Two languages, both directionsEvery design in English and Hebrew, right to left, with a switch on every page

  • Hers, and portableNo server, no cookies, no tracking; plain files she owns and can move anywhere
